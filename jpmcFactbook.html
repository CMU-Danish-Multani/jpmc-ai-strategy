01

The firm at a glance

JPMorgan Chase is the largest bank in the United States by assets. It does two different jobs under one roof. It is a retail bank holding the accounts, cards and mortgages of ordinary households. It is also a wholesale bank that trades securities, advises companies on takeovers, moves corporate cash worldwide, and manages money for institutions and wealthy families.

How a bank makes money. Two ways, and they behave differently. Spread income is the gap between what the bank earns lending money out and what it pays to hold deposits. It rises and falls with interest rates, which the bank does not control. Fee income is charged for doing something: advising on a deal, managing a fund, processing a payment. It is steadier but has to be won each time. In FY2025 the split was roughly 52 percent spread to 48 percent fee.
02

Income statement

Three years of revenue, expense and profit. Two bases appear throughout. Reported follows standard accounting rules. Managed adds back a tax adjustment so that tax-exempt lending can be compared with ordinary lending. The firm runs its business on the managed basis, so most segment reporting uses it.

03

Where the money goes

Noninterest expense is what it costs to run the bank, excluding the interest it pays out. Compensation is 57 percent of it. The line most people reach for when arguing about technology investment is the one that needs the most care.

A trap worth knowing before anyone cites it. The line called "Technology, communications and equipment" grew 12.2 percent in FY2025, which looks like accelerating technology investment. It is not. That line also contains depreciation on cars the bank leases out, which was 2,418 million dollars in FY2025 against 1,685 million in FY2024. Strip that out and real growth was 5.7 percent, which is lower than FY2024's adjusted 9.1 percent. There is also no disclosed technology budget anywhere in any filing. 04

The four segments

The bank reports as four businesses. The overhead ratio is expense divided by revenue, so lower is more efficient. The investment bank is both the largest earner and the most efficient. Asset and Wealth Management is the least efficient, because it is a people business.

Verification performed. The four segment revenues sum to 185,581 exactly, the four net income figures to 57,048 exactly, and the four expense figures to 95,640 exactly. All three ties are exact with no rounding residual. Recomputed independently, not taken on trust.
05

Balance sheet

What the bank owns and owes. A bank's balance sheet is unusual: customer deposits are a liability, because the bank owes that money back, and loans are an asset. The loans to deposits ratio shows how much of the deposit base has been lent out.

06

Regulatory capital

Common equity tier 1, or CET1, is the highest quality capital a bank holds: ordinary shareholders' money that absorbs losses first. Regulators require it as a percentage of risk weighted assets, which is total assets adjusted so that riskier lending counts for more. The gap between what the bank holds and what it must hold is the buffer available for lending, buybacks and dividends.

Why this bank's requirement is higher than a small bank's. JPMorgan Chase is designated a globally systemically important bank, which adds a surcharge on top of the base requirement, because a failure would damage the wider financial system. The surcharge is 2.5 percent under one calculation method and 4.5 percent under another.
07

Risk, credit costs and fraud

Two different losses matter here. Credit losses happen when a borrower does not repay, and are measured as the net charge-off rate. Operating losses are a separate audited line that the 10-K glossary defines as primarily fraud losses on deposit accounts and cards. That second line matters to this project, because it is the only audited number that a fraud strategy would move directly.

Why this line is strategically interesting. A fraud loss avoided is a loss not booked. It is subtracted from an audited expense line by the accounting itself, not by a management choice. Every other cost-side AI opportunity in this project produces a headcount saving, and the firm's own record shows those savings being redeployed rather than banked. That asymmetry is the reason the team's recommended vertical moved to consumer fraud.
08

Operating scale

The size of the machine. Headcount growth has flattened sharply while the branch network has kept expanding, which is a deliberate strategy rather than an accident.

09

Against the peer group

JPMorgan Chase is larger than the next two American banks combined on market value, and holds more deposits than any other. Note that investment banking fees are the one measure where the gap closes: Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are close competitors there despite being a fraction of the size overall.

10

Shareholder returns

What the bank hands back to its owners. The dividend has risen sharply, up 41 percent per share over two years.

11

What is not here

Silence is not zero. These are figures the research specifically looked for and could not find in public sources. Recording them protects anyone who assumes an absent number means the answer is nil.

The most consequential gap. No SEC filing discloses a total technology budget, capitalised internal-use software separately, or any AI-specific spend. Any statement about what JPMorgan Chase spends on AI is therefore an inference from something else, and must be labelled that way.
